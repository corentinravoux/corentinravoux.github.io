About

I am a CNRS researcher at the Laboratoire de Physique de Clermont Auvergne (LPCA), working on the large-scale structure of the Universe with spectroscopic and photometric surveys.

My PhD (Université Paris-Saclay, 2022) measured the matter distribution with the Lyman-α forest of eBOSS and of the first DESI data. I started working on peculiar velocities as a postdoc at CPPM in Marseille (2022–2023), and joined the CNRS in November 2023. My work sits at two ends of the same axis: the local Universe, where supernova velocities measure how structure grows today, and the high-redshift intergalactic medium, where the Lyman-α forest measures the matter power spectrum on the smallest scales cosmology can reach.

z ≈ 0.05 — the local Universe

Growth of structure from peculiar velocities

Galaxies fall towards mass. Measure that infall and you measure the growth rate of structure, through the combination fσ₈ — a test of gravity on cosmological scales.

Type Ia supernovae are the most precise distance indicators at low redshift — about 6% in distance per object, against about 20% for Tully–Fisher and fundamental-plane galaxies — so the residuals of their Hubble diagram carry the velocity field. I developed a generalised framework for field-level, likelihood-based inference of fσ₈ from velocity and density fields, released as flip and now used in DESI, ZTF and Rubin-LSST DESC. On realistic simulations of six years of ZTF supernovae and the DESI Bright Galaxy Survey (DR3), adding the galaxy density field reduces the uncertainty on fσ₈ by up to 50%, to about 10%, without bias.

For Rubin-LSST, simulations of the full survey show that fσ₈ can be measured to 10% over 0.02 < z < 0.14 in the most realistic case — photometric classification, non-Ia contamination, missing host redshifts — and to better than 18% in each of three redshift bins. I estimated how many host redshifts DESI and 4MOST will provide; intrinsic-scatter modelling turns out to be one of the main systematics.

At that precision, photometric calibration takes over. With the AuxTel team I added Gaia spectra as standard stars for measuring the atmospheric transmission above the Rubin Observatory, alongside the CALSPEC standards, and a master's internship derived the corrections that bring the two into agreement. A paper is in preparation.

z ≈ 0.1 — is the expansion the same everywhere?

Anisotropy and cosmic dipoles

The cosmological principle is an assumption, not a measurement. Supernovae can test it directly.

The same Hubble-diagram residuals that give the velocity field also let you fit the expansion rate as a function of direction. With Chloé Barjou-Delayre, whose PhD I co-supervised, we built a likelihood fit of an H₀ dipole and tested it on realistic simulations of the ZTF supernova sample. An injected dipole of 3 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ is recovered without bias, to 0.33 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ in amplitude and to 3.4° and 6.1° in right ascension and declination, whatever H₀ is assumed. Peculiar velocities are the main contaminant: a coherent bulk flow can mimic an expansion dipole, so the signal of one analysis is the systematic of the other.

A parallel test uses source counts: our motion should imprint a dipole of known amplitude on the number of distant sources across the sky. Radio galaxies and quasars do show a dipole in the direction of the CMB one, but larger than expected — from about 3σ to more than 5σ depending on the catalogue and the analysis, one of the sharper anomalies in cosmology today. With a master's student, I built a number-count simulator to prepare this test for Rubin-LSST; a first look at DESI DR1 showed that its incompleteness still dominates, so the spectroscopic test waits for the full survey.

z ≈ 0.3 – 2.3 — a standard ruler

Baryon acoustic oscillations with DESI

The sound horizon left a fixed length scale imprinted in the matter distribution. Measuring it at many redshifts, with galaxies and with the Lyman-α forest, turns it into a ruler for the expansion history.

Inside DESI I work on the Lyman-α side of that measurement — the catalogues and the analysis pipeline the forest correlations are built from — and co-lead the Lyman-α software topical group. That work runs through the DR1 and DR2 Lyman-α BAO analyses, part of the measurement that now favours evolving dark energy.

How far does non-linear physics move the peak? On the ACCEL² simulations, which I co-initiated and co-lead, an effective-field-theory analysis led by R. de Belsunce found shifts of the BAO scale of at most 0.3%, for the forest auto-correlation and for its cross-correlation with quasars — small enough to keep the forest a trustworthy ruler at z ≈ 2.3. The estimate entered the DESI DR2 Lyman-α BAO and full-shape analyses as their theoretical systematic.

z ≈ 2.5 — mapping, not just measuring

Tomographic maps, and the voids inside them

Every distant quasar is a backlight: the neutral hydrogen between us and it absorbs at the Lyman-α wavelength, redshifted, leaving a forest of absorption lines. With enough sightlines close enough together, the forest becomes a three-dimensional map.

From the eBOSS quasars of the SDSS Stripe 82 field — 220 deg², 37 sightlines per square degree — I reconstructed the flux-contrast field between the sightlines with a Wiener filter. The map covers 0.94 h⁻³ Gpc³ at 13 h⁻¹ Mpc resolution, the largest-volume three-dimensional map of matter fluctuations at high redshift. The pipeline is open, as lelantos.

A map lets you ask questions a power spectrum cannot: where are the underdense regions? We identified voids in the map and measured their cross-correlation with the forest. Its quadrupole carries the flow of matter out of the voids: redshift-space distortions detected at 8σ at a median redshift of 2.49, the first detection of flows around voids that far back, with a model that accounts for the sightlines being parallel.

z ≈ 2.2 – 4.2 — the intergalactic medium

The Lyman-α forest power spectrum

Along each sightline, the forest traces the matter distribution down to about a megaparsec — smaller scales than any galaxy survey can reach.

I lead the measurement of the one-dimensional Lyman-α power spectrum, P1D, with the Fast Fourier Transform estimator in DESI: first on the early data (26,330 quasar spectra), then on Data Release 1. Together with an independent quadratic estimator, the DR1 measurement is the most precise P1D to date. It constrains the small-scale matter power spectrum and, through it, the running of the primordial spectral index, neutrino masses, warm dark matter and the thermal history of the intergalactic medium.

The DR1 cosmological analysis measures the amplitude and slope of the linear power spectrum at z = 3, Δ²★ = 0.379 ± 0.032 and n★ = −2.309 ± 0.019, and, combined with the CMB and DESI BAO, tightens the constraints on Neff and on the running of the spectral index.

The measurement is only as good as the simulations behind it. I co-initiated and co-lead ACCEL², a grid of Nyx hydrodynamic simulations run on GPU supercomputers (Jean Zay, Perlmutter, Frontier), from 25 h⁻¹ kpc resolution up to a 640 h⁻¹ Mpc box. It showed that the redshift-space parameter of the forest converges with resolution rather than box size, gave the first clear detection of the BAO peak in a hydrodynamic simulation, and calibrates effective-field-theory models of the forest to better than 2% up to k = 2 h Mpc⁻¹.

Software

Code I build and maintain

Each measurement above comes with its software. My packages are open, documented and versioned, with a licence and a Zenodo DOI; the collaboration code I contribute to, such as picca for the DESI Lyman-α analyses, is open too.

flip

Field-level likelihood inference of fσ₈ from velocity and density fields: analytic wide-angle covariances, JAX acceleration, emulators and survey Fisher forecasts. Used in DESI, ZTF and Rubin-LSST DESC.

peculiar velocities p1desi

The DESI Lyman-α P1D measurement with the FFT estimator: noise, resolution and metal corrections, systematics and covariance, built on picca outputs.

DESI Lyα lelantos

Lyman-α tomography: from flux contrast to Wiener-filtered 3D maps of the intergalactic medium, and the voids inside them.

tomography lyapower

Fitting the 3D Lyman-α power spectrum and its BAO in Nyx hydrodynamic simulations, with splicing across resolutions.

simulations lyavoid

Cross-correlation of the Lyman-α forest with voids detected in tomographic maps.

voids growth_review

Compiled fσ₈, BAO and S₈ constraints with the plotting layer behind the review figures, plus an EFTCAMB modified-gravity backend.

compilation gaiaspec

Gaia low-resolution spectra loaded and formatted as standard stars for the AuxTel atmospheric-transmission pipeline.

calibration desidescsn

Which Rubin-LSST supernova hosts DESI and 4MOST can give a redshift: spectroscopic efficiencies from DC2 and survey target selections.

LSST × DESI lymanalpha-tutorials

Notebooks from the tutorials I teach in schools and summer schools, built around applications of the Lyman-α forest.
